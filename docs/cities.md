# The cities: what is known

Written by `scripts/build-data.ts` from `scripts/cities.ts`; do not edit it by hand. Every figure here is counted
from the two inputs: the Wikidata snapshot of 2026-11-11 (CC0) and Natural Earth 5.1.2's 10m Populated Places (public domain).

## The tiers

`capitals` holds every capital of any size; `1m`, `100k` and `15k` hold every city of that many people, capitals included, so a city can be in `capitals` and in one of the others. `citySet` keeps a city once.

## How a city is chosen

- **Wikidata**: the items that are an instance of a human settlement (Q486972 and every class below it through P279), have coordinates (P625) and a best-ranked population (P1082) of 15,000 or more: 82,364 items with that population and coordinates, of which 25,250 are a city, town or other settlement (4,612 more are only a metropolitan area, urban area or conurbation, and are left out; the rest are districts, counties and the like); every item that is the current capital (P36) of a country or a subdivision; and every item Natural Earth's places name. The queries are written in the snapshot file itself.
- **Natural Earth**: 7,336 places ("Historic place" left out). 7,159 name a Wikidata item (WIKIDATAID) that the snapshot holds; 27 name an item that is an area or a unit (Jamalpur naming Jamalpur District) and are matched again as if they named none; 31 more were matched by position (within 15 km, same country, same name); the 48 left have no Wikidata item here: 48 are cities of their own, with an id `ne:<Natural Earth's number>` (a capital, or 15,000 people or more); the others have no country, or are small and no capital, and are left out.
- **Which source wins**: Wikidata's label, point and population are kept where it has them. Natural Earth fills a Japanese name, a population or a point Wikidata does not have, and a capital where Wikidata names none; each city says which in its `source` field.
- **Population**: the best-ranked statement of the latest day (P585); of statements with no day, the largest. Natural Earth's `POP_MAX` has no day.
- **Subdivision**: the ISO 3166-2 code, from Wikidata's P300 on the city or the places it is in (P131), restricted to the first-level subdivisions kuni lists for its country; where there are several, the first in code order.
- **Areas**: Wikidata's human settlements include areas of several cities and the units that hold them. An item that is only a metropolitan area, an urban area, a conurbation, an urban agglomeration or a built-up area (by its class) is left out when the snapshot is made, and so is one whose English name ends in Province, County, Prefecture, Governorate, District, Regency, Oblast or Department, or names an area (metropolitan area, urban area, agglomeration, conurbation), or starts 'County of', 'Province of', 'District of', 'Municipality of', 'City of' or 'Greater' (Greater Sudbury, Greater Napanee and Greater Noida are cities and stay), or ends in a number (Prague 5, Sector 3, Municipio 2: a numbered district), or starts 'Metropolitan', 'Metro', 'Metropolis of', 'Commune' or 'Sectorul' (Metropolitan Toronto, Metro Manila, the Metropolis of Lyon, Burundi's communes, Chișinău's sectors), or names a regional municipality or a 'State of' (North East State of Somalia), or ends in 'Region' or 'Region of …' (Øresund Region, Kurdistan Region of Iraq; a name with a comma, a town told apart by its region, stays), 'Capital City' (Podgorica Capital City, the unit round the city), 'Urban Corridor' or 'New Area' (Binhai New Area), or ends in a unit below a district (Upazila, Thana, Tehsil, Taluka, Municipal Okrug, Subdistrict, Ward, Union, Arrondissement, County Borough, Regional Council or Commune: Gazipur Sadar Upazila; the word alone, Union in New Jersey, is a town's name and stays), unless it is a capital or Natural Earth names it. A few items are left out by name, each with its reason (NOT_CITIES in scripts/cities.ts: Moro, Papua New Guinea, at 6.3 million people). In Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Missouri, Nebraska and the Dakotas a township is a civil unit of its county with cities inside it: every township is left out (332; Kaw Township, round Kansas City), each id answering with the city of its own name, else the nearest within 15 km of a quarter of its people or more; elsewhere a township is the municipality people live in (Toms River, New Jersey; Clinton Township, Michigan) and stays. Some cities with such a name are lost; a city that Natural Earth names is kept. Then a unit or a part of a city that Wikidata names as a settlement is left out: a name ending in County, District, Subdistrict, Microdistrict, Raion, Governorate, Territory, Region or Area (a capital, a name with a comma, and a name in brackets stay), a numbered or named part ("Zone 7 of Milan", "third sector of Marseille"), and a municipality with a town of its own name within 25 km (Kodad municipality); its id answers with that town, else the city it is part of, else a city within 3 km, else the biggest within 15 km of twice its people (Xətai raion, Baku), else none (388 ids in all with the townships). Where Natural Earth names such a unit and no city stands apart, it is the town Natural Earth has (38: Yishui County is Yishui), with the town's point and people, not the county's.
- **Capitals**: a country has exactly one national capital (Natural Earth's "Admin-0 capital" among the items that are a capital of it, else the most populous Wikidata names): 241 cities are one. Another seat of government (Cape Town, Kyoto) is an `alternate` (11); the capital of a subdivision needs an ISO 3166-2 code kuni lists (4,031), and Natural Earth's subdivision capitals with none (294) are not capitals here. Every capital says what it is the capital of in `capitalOf`.
- **One city once**: two items with one name in one country within 15 km, one of them of 100,000 people or more, are one city; the one with a capital behind it, else Natural Earth's, else the more populous, is kept.
- **Urban area** (`metro`, 1,665 cities): the urban area a city heads, one source for each country. Canada: Statistics Canada's census metropolitan areas and census agglomerations, the estimate of 1 July 2025 (Table 17-10-0148-01), else the 2021 Census (Table 98-10-0005-01). The United States: the Census Bureau's metropolitan and micropolitan statistical areas on 1 July 2024. The other members of the OECD: its functional urban areas, the latest year it gives. China and India, whose own figure for a city is its whole municipality: their censuses' urban figures for their first cities, typed as facts with their source and date (`scripts/urban-facts.ts`: the urban resident population of China's twenty-one megacities in 2020; India's twenty largest urban agglomerations in 2011, on which Mumbai ranks ahead of Delhi, the larger today); their other cities rank after these, by Wikidata's metro where bigger, else their own figure. An area belongs to the city its name names first ("Ottawa - Gatineau" to Ottawa, "Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington" to Dallas), in its province or state, the most populous where two share the name; an area whose name is no city's is set by id in `scripts/metro-links.ts`, with the reason. 373 areas name no city kuni has (most are small Canadian agglomerations named for a region). The area's figure ranks a city even where it is below the city's own (Ulsan). Where a city heads none, Wikidata's metropolitan area where it is bigger than the city, an estimate (`metro.estimated`, `rankEstimated`), unless a bigger official area lies within 60 km, which holds it (Kyoto and Kobe, in Osaka's): then, and where there is none, the city's own figure (an estimate too in a country with no official areas). South Africa's metropolitan municipalities are set on their seats by name in `scripts/metro-links.ts` (eThekwini on Durban). Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan are under their own codes, whatever Wikidata's country.
- **Inside another city's area** (`inMetroOf`, 5,020 cities): a city that heads no area but lies inside another city's, ranked after every city that heads one. Set by id in `scripts/metro-links.ts` with the reason (Ecatepec in Mexico City's, Thane in Mumbai's, Giza in Cairo's, New York City's boroughs, every district of Bangkok); named in the Census Bureau's area (Fort Worth in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington); in Canada, a municipality of Statistics Canada's list of the municipalities of each area (Table 98-10-0003-01: Mississauga and Brampton are in Toronto's); else a city within 10 km plus 5 km for each square root of a million people of the nearest city whose area holds three times its own people or more, or in a country with no areas whose own figure of a million or more does. A city of a million people or more is never put inside another's for being near it (Yokohama, Kobe and Kyoto rank by their own). In China and India a city below the census list is ranked after it (`urbanAreaUnknown`, 6,704 cities).
- **Names**: English is Wikidata's label, else Natural Earth's; a city with neither is left out. Japanese is Wikidata's label (always preferred: it is curated by people), else Natural Earth's (not where it is written in hiragana for a place outside Japan, which marks a machine's guess), else `null`: never an English name copied in, and never made up; the few written for this package (`JA_NAMES` in `scripts/cities.ts`, each with its reason: East Jerusalem is 東エルサレム, not the whole city's エルサレム) say `kuni` as their source. The native name is the label in the country's official language (P37, language code P424; the first in code order that has a label, English left out), and Wikidata's native label (P1705, which often names a regional language: Kölle for Köln) only where there is none; it is `null` where it is the English name.
- **Time zone** (`timeZone`, `timeZoneEstimated`): the IANA zone the city keeps, for a page that shows the time there. 5,388 cities have Natural Earth's zone for the place (public domain; one that is not a zone IANA's zone.tab lists for the city's country is not used, 48 places, and the names tzdb has made links are read as the zones they are now: Asia/Rangoon is Asia/Yangon). Of the others, 11,699 are in a country with one zone, and 1,202 in a subdivision of a country with several for which Wikidata names the one zone it is in (`/zones`'s `subdivisionTimeZone`). Those are what the sources say, so `timeZoneEstimated` is false for 18,289 cities. For 11,693 it is true: the zone of the nearest city in the same subdivision that has one from Natural Earth, within 150 km, else the zone whose principal city in zone.tab is within 30 km, else the zone kuni chose for the city's subdivision, else the zone of the nearest subdivision's capital or middle. Kuni has no boundaries, so a city near the edge of a zone, or in a subdivision split between zones, can be given its neighbour's. A check: for the 2,376 cities that Natural Earth has a zone for and an estimate would have been made for (its own zone set aside, its neighbours' kept), the estimate gives the same clock (the same UTC offset in January and in July of 2026) for 98.1% and the same zone for 94.9%; the cities Natural Earth has are the larger ones, so the smaller towns an estimate is made for are probably less sure. 0 cities are in a country with no zone in zone.tab and have `null`.
- **Elevation** (`elevation`, 17,208 cities): Wikidata's best-ranked height above sea level (P2044, CC0), as Wikidata normalises it to metres, kept to the whole metre. A city with several values keeps the middle one only when they lie within 50 m of each other (1,044 cities had several; 286 were further apart and have `null`), and a value outside -430 to 5500 m is a mistake and is left out (0). It is never made up, and a city Wikidata gives none has `null`.

## What was left out

Of the 33,915 items in the snapshot: 694 had no English name (in Wikidata or Natural Earth), 9 no coordinates, 48 no country, 8 no population and were no capital, 1,309 had an English name of an area or an administrative unit (a metropolitan area, an urban area, a province, a county, a district) and no capital or Natural Earth place behind them, 113 were the same city as another item (Tokyo is two), and 1,566 fell below 15,000 people by the figure used (they were in because another statement was above it, or Natural Earth names them).

## What is missing

- **Japanese name**: 9,203 of 29,982 cities have none, in either source; the field is `null`.
- **Native name**: 16,127 cities have none or it is the English name; the field is `null`.
- **Subdivision**: 1,259 cities have none (Wikidata does not place them in a first-level subdivision kuni lists).
- **Elevation**: 12,774 of 29,982 cities have none; the field is `null`.

Natural Earth release 5.1.2; Wikidata read 2026-11-11.
